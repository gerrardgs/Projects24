# Evidence register

Every finding the other files in this skill rely on, with where it comes from, how strong it is and what it cannot tell you. Other files cite entries as [E-01]. A claim without an entry here does not belong in this skill ([03-verification](../../../rules/03-verification.md)).

Columns:

- **Type**: `standard` (a standards body or health authority), `peer-reviewed`, `report` (not peer-reviewed), `practitioner` (a research team of a design consultancy or platform), `theory` (a model or a book).
- **Strength** for applying it to interface design: `A` a standard, or a finding with direct use and no known dispute; `B` credible published research whose limits are stated; `C` a lens or a weak or indirect result, to be tested before use.
- **Read as**: `page` (the page text was opened on the check date), `abstract` (only the abstract or a search summary of the paper was read; the full paper was not), `secondary` (only reports about the source were read).
- **Checked**: the date the source was last looked at. `node assets/check-evidence.mjs` warns when an entry is older than 365 days: re-open the source, then change the date.

## Not established here

These come up often in design talk. Nothing in this register supports them, so do not use them as facts. To use one, research it, add an entry and cite it.

- Generational or age-band taste ("Gen Z wants..."), and taste by nationality as a design rule. E-07 shows group tendencies that differ by country, age, gender and education, and E-24 shows why national-culture scores are risky.
- Personality-trait predictions of aesthetic preference, and personalising an interface from a guessed personality.
- Meanings of colours by country beyond the general pattern in E-08.
- The golden ratio and similar ratios as the cause of preference.
- Any statement that a trend "is what users want now". A trend is a dated observation about designers.
- Conversion or revenue effects of a visual style.
