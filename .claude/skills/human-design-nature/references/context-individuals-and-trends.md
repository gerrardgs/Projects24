# Context, individuals and trends

Taste lives in situations. The same person wants different things on a train, at a desk, in an emergency. Cited as [E-nn] ([evidence-register](evidence-register.md)).

## 1. Context of use outranks taste

Ask, for each primary screen: who, where, on what device, with what attention, under what ambient light, how often, how stressed. Then pick the mode ([design-passes](../../ui-design-and-prototyping/references/design-passes.md)):

| Context | Leans toward |
| :--- | :--- |
| Daily tool, long sessions, expert users | Low complexity, familiar patterns, quiet colour, dense but legible, both light and dark themes [E-03, E-17] |
| Rare, high-stakes task (money, health, legal) | Calm affect, plain words, visible progress and recovery, no decoration competing with content [E-11, E-14] |
| Quick use in motion or poor light | Large targets, strong contrast, short flows, offline tolerance |
| Persuasion or discovery | Room for novelty and a signature, with legible controls [E-04, E-16] |
| Reading and learning | Measure and rhythm for comprehension first |
| Children or older users | Larger targets, simpler gestures, more spacing, more forgiveness [E-19, E-21] |

## 2. Individuals differ more than groups do

- Group tendencies exist [E-07] but individuals scatter widely around them. A tendency tells you what to test, never what to assume.
- Personality traits and aesthetic taste: nothing in this register supports predicting a person's taste from a guessed personality. Do not personalise by inferred traits, and do not write personas that smuggle in stereotypes. Personalisation should come from choices the user made or behaviour they agreed to share, with consent ([06-security](../../../rules/06-security.md) section 8).
- Offer a few meaningful settings instead of a settings maze: theme, text size, density, motion, language.

## 3. Trends

- A trend is a dated observation about what designers are making, not a measure of what people need. Record the date when you cite one, and ask what the trend serves here.
- Trends can cost real usability: the move to flat interfaces with weak clickability cues slowed people down in a controlled study [E-16, B]. Follow a trend only where it passes the purpose test of [anti-slop-filter](../../anti-slop-filter/SKILL.md) and the user can still find and operate everything.
- Familiarity builds liking over time [E-06]; novelty pays when typicality is kept [E-04]. A trend adopted by everyone becomes the category's typical look, which is also why an identity built only on the current trend is hard to keep distinct ([project-design-identity](../../project-design-identity/SKILL.md)).

## 4. When the audience is unknown

Say so in the identity brief. Default to the mode of the surface, a calm and legible baseline that follows system settings, and plan the first research step. Do not fill the gap with invented personas, statistics or "users love..." sentences ([AGENTS.md](../../../../AGENTS.md) non-negotiable 1).
