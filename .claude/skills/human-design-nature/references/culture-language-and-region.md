# Culture, language and region

Taste and habit differ between groups of people. The honest use of that fact is to research the actual audience, not to apply national stereotypes. Cited as [E-nn] ([evidence-register](evidence-register.md)).

## 1. What is supported

- Visual preferences measured on 430 websites by nearly 40,000 people differed with country, age, gender and education: for example colourfulness and complexity were liked differently across countries, education correlated negatively with liking colourful and complex sites, and women rated saturated colours higher [E-07, B]. These are tendencies of groups in a self-selected online sample.
- Colour-emotion associations are broadly shared and locally tinted [E-08, B].
- Brand personality dimensions differ between cultures [E-09, B].
- Translated text is longer than English, most for short strings: up to 300 percent for strings of ten characters or fewer, about 130 percent for long text [E-22, B].

## 2. What is not supported

- Reading a country's design from a national-culture score. The Hofstede scores come from an employee survey at one company around 1967 to 1973, and their analysis has been challenged [E-24, B]. Do not derive "this country wants dense interfaces" from them.
- Generalising from a group tendency to a person. A tendency says what to test first, never what an individual wants.

## 3. What to do

1. **Name the audiences**, with where they live, which languages and scripts they read, which devices and connections they use, and the context of use. Get this from the owner or from research ([ux-research-and-strategy](../../ux-research-and-strategy/SKILL.md)); do not invent it.
2. **Plan for language**: right-to-left layouts (mirror direction, keep numerals and embedded Latin text correct), scripts that need more line height, no text inside images, labels that can grow by 100 to 200 percent, and dates, numbers and currency formatted by locale ([break-ui](../../frontend-testing-and-visual-qa/references/break-ui.md)).
3. **Check meanings locally**: colours, icons, gestures, imagery, numbers and symbols can mean something else elsewhere. Ask people who live there, and see [13-visual-assets-and-licensing](../../../rules/13-visual-assets-and-licensing.md) for people and places in imagery.
4. **Respect local conventions that users already hold**: payment methods, address and name formats, date order, and the apps people use every day set expectations that a new product inherits.
5. **Test with the audience.** Where the audience is mixed, give control (language, text size, theme, density) instead of picking one taste.

## 4. A note for Indonesian-language products

No register entry yet covers Indonesian audiences specifically. When a project targets Indonesia, do local research (devices and connection quality, languages and regional languages, payment and messaging habits, religious and public calendar), add what you can source as new register entries with dates, and write the rest as open questions in the project's identity brief. Do not import assumptions from other markets.
