# Study cards: twenty design ideas to observe, imitate and modify

Each card is an idea found in studied pages, written as a technique to learn, not a template to copy. They are rewritten in our own words from the twenty "directions" of [leonxlnx/taste-skill](https://github.com/Leonxlnx/taste-skill) (MIT, commit `12af471`, read 2026-11-02). That project distilled them from a study of 247 pages drawn from two design galleries, judged by model reviewers from still frames; its author notes the sample "is fashion as much as quality". Treat every card as a lens of strength C ([evidence register](../../human-design-nature/references/evidence-register.md) scale): a pattern that worked on some pages, not a finding about what people prefer.

How to use a card: read **Observe**, practise **Imitate** in a scratch page with your own content, then follow **Modify** until the result is yours and passes the [identity check](../../project-design-identity/SKILL.md). Record the use in the project's study ledger ([SKILL.md](../SKILL.md)). **Watch** lists what makes the idea fail.

None of these cards names a font, colour or component to use. That is deliberate: a published "use this instead" list becomes the next wave of generic pages within months. The replacement is a procedure: derive the choice from the subject.

### S-01 The name as a monument
- **Observe:** the name is the headline, set as large as the viewport, cropped by an edge or stacked, with one small sentence beside it.
- **Imitate:** treat the letters of the name as material: a picture sits inside a counter, the name repeats like a reel, or it splits to two corners.
- **Modify:** ask what this subject can do to its own name. Let the answer come from the subject, not from the effect.
- **Fits:** studios, hospitality, portfolios. **Watch:** a long name set small is just a logo; stack or crop names over about 12 characters; do not add a second big headline or decorate the letters.

### S-02 The sentence is the layout
- **Observe:** one short sentence at poster scale is the composition, and something from the subject sits where a word would be.
- **Imitate:** write the sentence first, find the word that can be shown instead of said, and put a picture, a live fragment or an object there.
- **Modify:** the showing must be true to the subject (a real fragment of the product, a real object), not a decoration.
- **Fits:** agencies, tools, causes. **Watch:** the sentence must fit two lines on a phone; one slot per sentence; do not fake the device by styling a word.

### S-03 Pinned poster
- **Observe:** one element stays put (a title, a frame) while what is behind or beside it changes with each scroll step.
- **Imitate:** pin the element that says what the series is and let its members pass beneath.
- **Modify:** choose what is fixed from the subject (the line-up, the collection) and keep the run short.
- **Fits:** series: portraits, line-ups, collections. **Watch:** the title must be legible over every panel; a pin longer than about three screens feels like a trap; a phone needs its own version. Provide a non-pinned, reduced-motion version.

### S-04 Colour chapters
- **Observe:** each section is a chapter with its own flat colour, bound by one typeface and one grid.
- **Imitate:** order the colours like a book: a strong opener, a quiet second, a surprise, a close.
- **Modify:** each colour comes from the subject and means that chapter (a product colourway, a category).
- **Fits:** campaigns, education, events. **Watch:** check contrast on every field; no cards on the fields; at most about six colours; neighbouring fields should differ in lightness, not only hue.

### S-05 The place carries it
- **Observe:** for a place, one full-screen photograph is the argument and the design steps back.
- **Imitate:** edit hard: choose the single frame that makes someone want to be there, then do not cover it.
- **Modify:** the picture must be of this place, licensed ([13-visual-assets-and-licensing](../../../rules/13-visual-assets-and-licensing.md)).
- **Fits:** hospitality, real estate, architecture. **Watch:** typography cannot rescue a weak photograph; no headline, paragraph and two buttons on top; no booking bar covering the view.

### S-06 The page is the object
- **Observe:** the page is built as the printed or physical thing the subject already has: an invitation, a menu, a ticket, a sleeve, a receipt, a folder.
- **Imitate:** borrow the object's proportions, its typographic manners and its way of grouping information.
- **Modify:** pick an object that truly belongs to the subject, or it is a costume; shape its edges with fills and clipping, not an outlined rectangle.
- **Fits:** events, restaurants, culture. **Watch:** keep text readable on a phone, where the object becomes full width.

### S-07 One motif
- **Observe:** one shape from the subject runs the whole system: buttons, image masks, loaders, bands.
- **Imitate:** find the simplest geometry in the product, logo, object or the headline's word, and use only that, at three or four sizes.
- **Modify:** you must be able to say in one clause where the motif comes from.
- **Fits:** products and brands with a visible object. **Watch:** one means one; a circle here, a hexagon there and a blob behind is the generated look this replaces.

### S-08 The product performs
- **Observe:** software is shown doing its job: small, true, working fragments instead of a description.
- **Imitate:** find the one interaction that is the product and make the hero that interaction.
- **Modify:** use real content and real behaviour from this product; mark the fragment so its own conventions apply inside it.
- **Fits:** tools, apps. **Watch:** a fake interface of grey bars and circles is the worst outcome; if you cannot build a believable fragment, choose another idea.

### S-09 Quiet instrument
- **Observe:** radical understatement: a headline barely larger than body text, a narrow column, small exact demonstrations, a great deal of space.
- **Imitate:** carry restraint through completely; the restraint is the claim.
- **Modify:** keep column edges and vertical rhythm exact so the emptiness looks decided.
- **Fits:** simple, finished tools. **Watch:** one loud element breaks it; not for subjects that need emotion or photography; quiet is not thin or low contrast.

### S-10 The object, large
- **Observe:** one photographed object is scaled until the viewport crops it, with small text beside it.
- **Imitate:** the crop is the idea; show the object closer than seems reasonable.
- **Modify:** needs a product photograph you actually have.
- **Fits:** physical products. **Watch:** a small centred product with space around it is a catalogue thumbnail; busy backgrounds cannot sit on a field; keep text off the focal area.

### S-11 Specimen catalogue
- **Observe:** each item alone on a flat colour, same scale and angle, named plainly, like a field guide.
- **Imitate:** treat ordinary items as worth looking at; let the catalogue be the menu or range.
- **Modify:** the tile colour and the naming come from the subject.
- **Fits:** shops, bakeries, makers. **Watch:** needs at least about six items or it is a feature row; labels outside the tiles; no shadows, no hover lift.

### S-12 Dressed as something else
- **Observe:** the subject is art-directed as if it belonged to a more vivid category.
- **Imitate:** name the borrowed category in one word, ask which feeling the audience misses in this kind of product, and take that category's manners completely.
- **Modify:** content must stay clear (what, cost, how to get it); do not borrow from the category you are escaping.
- **Fits:** finance, tools, health. **Watch:** borrowing without a stated category is just loud; do not copy another brand's marks.

### S-13 Contact sheet
- **Observe:** everything at once, small, in strict rows, with all text at one size; the volume is the statement.
- **Imitate:** let the visitor choose; set one consistent frame size.
- **Modify:** only with real work to show.
- **Fits:** photographers, studios with range. **Watch:** under about 20 frames it looks unfinished; no big headline above; on a phone wrap frames instead of letting rows run off the edge.

### S-14 Printed report
- **Observe:** laid out like a well-edited report or feature: a masthead image, a tight oversized title, a strict column axis, solid colour blocks.
- **Imitate:** reuse one exact axis everywhere and hold text to a real reading measure.
- **Modify:** colour in solid blocks from a small set, as a two-colour print job would.
- **Fits:** findings, manifestos, programmes. **Watch:** a loose axis reads as random indentation; on a phone the axis collapses to the margin and side notes move inline.

### S-15 Loud and flat
- **Observe:** the directness of a street poster: flat saturated colour, the heaviest type, blunt statements, cut-out photographs.
- **Imitate:** write the page as things a person would say aloud, then set them at full volume.
- **Modify:** the control is in the grid: two or three colours and exact alignment.
- **Fits:** subjects with a voice. **Watch:** headlines still fit two lines; no outlines, offset shadows or sticker confetti; rotate only two or three images, never text blocks.

### S-16 Split stage
- **Observe:** half the screen never moves (a photograph, an object) while a calm column scrolls beside it.
- **Imitate:** keep the feeling present while the reader gets the facts.
- **Modify:** change what the reading half holds from section to section.
- **Fits:** restaurants, portfolios, products. **Watch:** the reading column must be genuinely well set; no cards in it; check the fixed image at short heights; monotony was the common complaint.

### S-17 Well-kept board
- **Observe:** the page is one board of tiles fitted edge to edge, each tile one true thing; no hero, no sequence of bands.
- **Imitate:** vary what tiles hold: a name, a statement, a photograph, a number, a link.
- **Modify:** only with many real small things to show.
- **Fits:** studios, multi-capability products. **Watch:** filled tiles, no outlines or shadows; no icon-plus-blurb in every tile; design the phone board as its own layout.

### S-18 Photographs at uneven sizes
- **Observe:** pictures placed like prints on a gallery wall: different sizes and positions, small light text between.
- **Imitate:** make unevenness deliberate: every edge lands on a column line and offsets come from a small set of values.
- **Modify:** the pictures must be strong and licensed.
- **Fits:** hospitality, architecture, fashion. **Watch:** uneven is not random; on a phone keep the rhythm with alternating widths.

### S-19 Dark room
- **Observe:** one dark field top to bottom, with lit exhibits: photographs and objects each the brightest thing on its screen.
- **Imitate:** darkness as a room, not a theme switch; light, small, sparse type.
- **Modify:** choose low-key images.
- **Fits:** atmosphere and material: an evening restaurant, a record. **Watch:** grey on black fails contrast quickly (running text needs strong contrast); no glow, neon or coloured blobs; dark does not mean empty. A light theme must still exist where users need it ([human-design-nature](../../human-design-nature/references/age-ability-and-neurodiversity.md) section 2).

### S-20 Type and nothing else
- **Observe:** the first screen is only words, colour and space; pictures arrive later, large and few.
- **Imitate:** carry the point of view through scale, measure and one committed typographic idea.
- **Modify:** write real sentences; this is the honest answer when nothing is worth photographing.
- **Fits:** tools, agencies. **Watch:** weak copy has nowhere to hide; vary the set pieces; keep headings to two lines.
