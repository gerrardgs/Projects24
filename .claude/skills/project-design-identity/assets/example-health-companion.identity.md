# Design identity: Example Health Companion

> A fictional example that shows the shape of a brief. It is not a real project, and its audience facts are illustrative.

## Audience and context

Illustrative: parents of young children tracking appointments and measurements on a phone, often tired or worried, in Bahasa Indonesia and English, on mid-range Android devices over unreliable connections. Sources: none (example). Unknown: reading ability range, share of older caregivers.

## Personality and feeling

Sincere and competent. The target feeling is pleasant and calm, never activated: no urgency colours or countdowns outside real emergencies.

## Signature decisions

- Colour: a muted teal (hue 190) with a warm neutral and a small amber accent (hue 45); status is never colour alone.
- Type: a soft serif for headings with a plain humanist sans for text; generous size and spacing.
- Shape and layout: soft corners, a single-column stacked layout, airy density.
- Imagery: hand-drawn illustration for empty states and milestones; no stock photographs of people.
- Voice: warm and plain, one idea per sentence, no blame in errors.
- Motion: states only; a gentle fade for the single milestone moment, with a reduced-motion variant.

## Not like

Not like clinical dashboards (cool white, dense tables, dashboard layout), and not like the other example in this folder, which is editorial, serif-and-red, photographic and dense.

## Studied references

ST-01: the restrained single-column pattern, kept for the check-in screen and changed with larger text, a wider column and one illustration ([design-study](../../design-study/SKILL.md)).

## Evidence and rationale

Calm, pleasant target feeling [E-11, B]. Colour never carries meaning alone [E-18, A]. Large targets and plain words for tired and older caregivers [E-19, A; E-20, A]. Labels allowed to grow for translation [E-22, B]. Warm illustration instead of photographs is a taste choice with no evidence behind it: test with caregivers.

## Open questions

Which illustration style do caregivers find trustworthy? Test three styles in a first-click and a five-second test.
